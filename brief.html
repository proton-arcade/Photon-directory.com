Design brief for a directory of offline sites

The hub should read like a notice board somebody keeps tidy: dark room, lamp on, blue paper, red pin. Everything you can click is ringed in yellow so there is never a guessing game, and everything that is not clickable stays quiet.

It is a directory, so the listing is the design. Pages are built out of rows, tables and notes rather than cards, and the only ornament a page is allowed is a drawing it is still waiting for.

01

Tone

Plain-spoken, first person singular or plural, willing to admit what a page cannot do. The media player has a section called “three things this player will not do”. The hub says outright that the drawings are missing rather than shipping a grey rectangle and hoping. Sentences vary in length on purpose. Some are long and wander through the reasoning. Some don't.

No filler. Every byte count, file name, date and codec claim is a real number that can be checked against the folder, and tools/check.py fails the build if the manifest and the files disagree.

Text is left-aligned, always, and pushed off the page edge by an indent. Nothing is centred except the column itself.

03

Type

Two families, both installed on every machine since forever: Georgia for the logo, headings and the numbers in a HUD; Courier New for body copy and every piece of interface. Serif over monospace is the oldest pairing on the web and it still reads at 16px on a bad screen, which is the screen this hub is for. No webfonts, because a hub that needs a CDN to look like itself is not offline.

Hierarchy is strict and there is nothing in between: 38 page title, 24 section head in yellow, 17 item head, 16 body, 14 table and button text, 13 asides, 12 kickers with 3px tracking, 11 sizes and micro-labels. A component that needs a new size needs a new rule in this table first.

Body copy is capped at 62ch (58ch in a narrow column) and sits left. Line-height is 1.65 everywhere, including tables, because cramped tables are the classic way an old-web page reads as a spreadsheet nobody maintains.

04

Space and alignment

The column is 860px and centred; the text inside it is not. Sections use three gaps — 26px tight, 54px normal, 96px loose — chosen by how much of a breath the section needs, so the rhythm is uneven on purpose and you can feel where a chapter ends. Indents step in at 26px and 58px. Corners are 2px, on everything that has a corner. Everything else is square.

Gaps between interactive things are 8–14px, never 4, and there is 28px of air on the sides of the column (16px under 640px) so a ring around a button never touches its neighbour.

05

What is allowed to move

Three things, and each one because it is doing a job:

  • The notice strip scrolls, because a notice board really does change. It has a button on the homepage, because a scrolling strip is also the thing people most like to stop.
  • A directory prints. When the index, a search result set or the game shelf is put on the screen, the rows come in top to bottom at 55ms each — that is the “listing” a lookup actually is. It runs once, on paint. It is never attached to scrolling.
  • Game state. A ball moves, a flood fill opens, a lamp flips. Nothing eases.

Everything else is static: no scroll reveals, no hover choreography, no parallax, no skeletons, no spinners. Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce the print collapses to one paint and the strip still scrolls, because a marquee is content, not decoration — and you can stop it.

Refused on purpose

RefusedBecause
Gradient buttonsA ring in one yellow already says “press me”.
Glass and blurText on a blurred background is how a page starts a accessibility lawsuit.

Card grids, four acrossThe unit here is a row. Six identical cards would be a template with the serial numbers filed off.
Three-tier pricingThe hub has no price.
TestimonialsOne field note, in its own box, on the front page. Not three in a row.
Newsletter boxThere is nothing to subscribe to and no service to send it.
Cookie bannerNo cookies, so no banner to refuse. tools/check.py fails the build if document.cookie, a beacon or an analytics script appears anywhere in the folder.
Icon packsGlyphs are ↑ arrows and box-drawing characters, and the logo is text with a red bar on it.
Scroll-triggered animationSee 05. A listing prints; a section does not fade up at you.

The rule behind the list

A page is allowed exactly as much personality as it needs to tell you what it is. Which is why the hub has a marquee, an honest visitor dial, a webring made of its own pages, and no illustrations it cannot draw itself.

Old web, kept honestly

Flat file names, relative links, tables for tabular data, a <marquee> that can be switched off, and a 404.html that hands you the index instead of an apology. Nothing ironic about it: those constraints are why the hub is 310 KB of text and one 17 MB game, and why both numbers are printed in the folder listing instead of guessed.

07

How it is checked

CommandWhat it proves
node tools/test-logic.js 293 assertions over the deciding logic: snake growth and tail rules, the minesweeper flood and win conditions, all four 2048 directions, maze connectivity on nine board sizes, Lights Out solvability, Pong physics, and the container prober against hand-built Matroska, MP4 and AVI bytes plus 400 random buffers.
node tools/test-dom.js 348 assertions over nine pages in a headless DOM: nav consistency, no console errors, the directory prints and packs its caret away, pins survive a reload, all seven games mount and tear down, the player wires up its deck, the notes autosave what was typed.
python3 tools/check.py The folder agrees with itself: every link resolves, every manifest byte count is current, no placeholder text survives, no external resource is referenced, tags balance, every img has real alt text, the Eaglercraft file is intact.

Run all three before pushing, and tools/sizes.py first: the front page prints a byte count for every file in the folder, and that is the table that goes stale. They are the reason the hub can say “400 random buffers” without hoping.

How editing works

This page and the copy on every other page are ordinary HTML: change the text, keep the tags. Anything structural is in style.css at the top, and the components below it are additive, so a new page can use .wrap, .btn, .dir, .draw, .box and .note without touching the rules above. A new site in the hub is one entry in js/index-data.js and one file next to index.html; the index, the search and the webring pick it up on their own.
