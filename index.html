The Work

DIT.EDITFour things we finished and one we didn't

DIT.EDIT01 — Redbird Feed Store, catalog + site

DIT.EDITForty years of seed inventory lived in a three-ring binder behind the register. We typed it out, priced it, and put it on one long page that a farmer can read on a cracked phone in a truck with one bar of signal. Sixty-one kilobytes. Orders went up; nobody mentioned the website.

DIT.EDIT02 — The Thursday Reading Room

DIT.EDITA single page listing what is being read aloud, who is reading it, and where to sit. It updates by hand every Wednesday night. We were asked twice to add a calendar widget and said no twice.

DIT.EDIT03 — Marisol's archive of union pamphlets

DIT.EDITEleven hundred scans, each one transcribed so the text can be searched and quoted. The hardest part was not the code. It was deciding how to spell names that three different typesetters spelled three different ways in 1974.

DIT.EDIT04 — The one we didn't finish

DIT.EDITA reading app with margins you could write in. We got eight months deep before admitting the thing we loved about margins was the paper. We refunded half and kept the notes. They are in the cabinet that sticks.

DIT.EDITWhat it costs

DIT.EDITSmall site, up to six pages: $2,400. Anything with an archive, a catalog or a thousand scans: starts at $6,000 and we'll tell you the real number after the paper stage. Zine printing is billed at cost plus the price of two lunches.

DIT.EDITIf you run a library, a food pantry or a picket line, ask anyway. We keep two slots a year for that and they are usually the work we are proudest of.

DIT.EDIT“They asked what my customers were doing with their hands when they'd be looking at the site. Nobody had ever asked me that. Turns out the answer was: holding a goat.”
